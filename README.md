# hems-synth

A seeded generator of Irish households with solar, a battery and EV charging at five-second resolution. It writes the same file shapes a home energy management pilot produces (a Home Assistant InfluxDB export per entity, a Sigenergy-style five-minute export, an ESB-Networks-HDF-style half-hourly file), re-implements the pilot's loading and cleaning rules from their docstrings, and publishes clean marts in Parquet and DuckDB with a tariff-cost table for eleven Irish smart-meter plans. The generator knows the truth, so the loader and every later project can measure their own error against it.

No real household data was used. Every site, station prefix, MPRN and meter serial in `config/sites.yaml` is made up, and the only inputs are seeds and the public tariff pages listed in `LICENCE_CHECK.md`.

## What it simulates

* **Load**: a base load with weekday and weekend daily shapes, a winter factor, random kettle, oven, washing machine and dishwasher cycles, a fridge cycle, and EV sessions on some nights (7.4 kW until a nightly target is met).
* **PV**: clear-sky irradiance from latitude, day of year and clock time, an AR(1) cloud factor at five-minute steps with a daily mean, clipped at the inverter AC limit.
* **Battery**: self-consumption dispatch, charge from surplus up to the power limit, discharge to cover a deficit, state of charge between 5 % and 100 %.
* **Grid**: load minus PV plus charge minus discharge, split into import and export.

Truth is written to `data/<run>/truth/<site>/` as five-second, half-hourly and daily Parquet files (Europe/Dublin days and half-hours) with the EV sessions and the artefact log.

## Loader

`src/hems_synth/loader.py` keeps the pilot's names and signatures: `read_entity`, `read_states`, `_minute_mean`, `_despike`, `_counter_to_cumulative`, `load_site` for the Sofar export, `prefixes`, `entities`, `_clean_daily_counter`, `_held`, `load_site_sigen` for the Sigenergy export, `read_esb_hdf` for the HDF file, and `ev_sessions` for the EV rule (above 5,000 W, sessions closer than 10 minutes merged, 350 W base subtracted, 12 kW import limit). A run of 15 minutes or more without a point from any entity becomes NaN, never held values. Two choices are documented in the module: the Sofar `import` and `export` columns come from the lifetime counters, which never reset and so never lose the last step of a day (the day counters feed `totals`, and the integrated grid power is kept as `import_power` and `export_power`), and the Sigenergy `export` column comes from the station export counter (the integrated grid power is kept as `export_power`).

## Mart schema

`data/<run>/marts/*.parquet` and `hems.duckdb`:

| Table | Columns |
|---|---|
| `sites` | the profile columns of `config/sites.yaml`, no names |
| `energy_30min` | `site_id, ts_utc, ts_local, source` (`truth` or `export`), `import_kwh, export_kwh, pv_kwh, load_kwh, batt_charge_kwh, batt_discharge_kwh, ev_kwh, soc_pct_end` |
| `daily` | the same flows per local day plus `self_consumption_pct, ev_sessions` |
| `tariff_cost` | `site_id, month, plan_key, days, kwh_import, kwh_export, kwh_day, kwh_night, kwh_peak, kwh_ev, unit_eur, standing_eur, pso_eur, export_credit_eur, net_eur`, from the truth rows |
| `tariffs` | the eleven plans with rates, windows, standing charge, export rate, discount, effective date, research date and source URLs |
| `esb_30min` | the HDF-style file read with `clock="local"` (the ambiguous hour dropped) |
| `truth_vs_export` | per site, day and flow: truth kWh, loader kWh, difference and percentage |

`ts_local` is naive Irish wall-clock time, `ts_utc` is UTC.

## Tariffs

`data/tariffs/plans.json` holds eleven current smart-meter plans from Electric Ireland, Energia, SSE Airtricity, Bord Gáis Energy and Pinergy, researched on 28 October 2026 from the suppliers' own price pages (URLs and effective dates in the file), with the 9 % VAT rate and the 2026/27 PSO levy and their sources. Bands are local clock hours, a window whose start is after its end wraps midnight, priority EV, then peak, then night, then day. `rate_vector` gives the 48 half-hourly rates of a day and `bill` the kWh per band, unit cost, standing charge, PSO levy, export credit and net cost. A weekday-only peak is modelled every day, which the plan note states.

```
C:/Users/nagar/anaconda3/python.exe -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\pip install -e .[dev]
.venv\Scripts\python -m pytest -q
.venv\Scripts\python -m hems_synth smoke
.venv\Scripts\python -m hems_synth generate --sites S01,S02,S03 --start 2026-01-01 --days 365 --seed 42 --artefacts --out data
.venv\Scripts\python -m hems_synth marts --in data --out data/marts
.venv\Scripts\python -m hems_synth validate --in data --marts data/marts
.venv\Scripts\python -m hems_synth serve --db data/marts/hems.duckdb
.venv\Scripts\python -m build --wheel
```

`serve` is a read-only FastAPI over the DuckDB file with `GET /sites`, `GET /usage`, `GET /tariff-cost` and `GET /healthz`. No key is needed anywhere. `costcap.py` is the workspace's cost ledger and is unused here because this unit makes no paid call.

See `LICENCE_CHECK.md`. No personal data is used. Everything under `data/` except `data/tariffs/plans.json` is generated from seeds, and the tariff file holds public prices with their source pages.

## Method

The five-second truth is simulated first, then each exporter applies the device's sampling, sensor noise, write-on-change rule, sign convention, dead band and counter behaviour, and injects the documented artefacts with a log. The loader reads only the exported files, never the truth. The validation joins loader output and truth per day and per half-hour, checks every logged artefact was handled, reads the HDF file both ways round the clock change, and compares the 48-slot tariff vector with a brute-force per-sample cost. The marts are written to Parquet and loaded into DuckDB from those files, and the two are summed against each other.

## Limits

* Twenty simulated days for two sites in the smoke run. A full year for three sites takes a few minutes and is the intended input for the later units.
* The weather is synthetic. Irradiance is geometric with a random cloud process, so seasonal yields are plausible, not measured.
* The loader's day-counter rules are re-implemented from one-paragraph docstrings and may differ in detail from the pilot's code. Where a rule left a choice open, the choice is stated in the docstring.
* The HDF column names are verified against public descriptions and a public reader, not against a real download.
* Tariff rates move often. The file records the research date and the page each number came from, and two Electric Ireland plans are only published with a discount included.
